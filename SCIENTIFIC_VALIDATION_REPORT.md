## What the present rule validates

For each candidate, a three-bit signature says whether each zone root can reach that site through the validated directed HydroRIVERS graph. With `r` reachable roots among `n` hypotheses, the score `r × (n − r)` counts pairs that would have different outcomes under an **ideal binary reachability assumption**. A score of 2 is the maximum for three zones. It is not expected information gain, a detection probability, or evidence of a biological source.

The generator considers validated upstream reaches, excludes Site A and unreachable or unusable graph locations, groups reaches by signature, excludes zero-score groups, and chooses the nearest-to-A reach within each useful signature as a representative. Its four Wigger representatives are:

| Reach | Signature Z1/Z2/Z3 | Score | Distance to A (km) | Registered follow-up site |
| ---: | :---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 20451169 | 001 | 2 | 23.936 | C |
| 20450127 | 010 | 2 | 19.226 | B |
| 20446568 | 011 | 2 | 2.286 | No |
| 20447392 | 100 | 2 | 3.586 | No |

The persisted decision instead evaluates the **registered** Wigger follow-up sites B, C, and D. D is reach 20448315 on the shared trunk, not the nearest generated representative of signature 011. All three registered sites score 2, so the actual decision is **TIE: B, C, D**. The fourth generated representative is not a fourth registered site; neither its shorter distance nor its equal score justifies changing the decision. An incomplete validated network produces `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`; no candidate that separates any hypothesis produces `ABSTAIN`.

Field accessibility, permission, safety, assay sensitivity, transport, decay, and cost are unknown. A distance or quality tie-breaker would require a new versioned rule with defensible measurements. The available evidence does not support one, so the tie remains.

## Historical validation boundary

The Carraro sources contain 15 station locations and repeated *F. sultana* and *T. bryosalmonae* measurements. The reviewed HydroRIVERS crosswalk classifies one station `VERIFIED`, seven `SUPPORTED`, one `AMBIGUOUS`, and six `NOT_VERIFIED`. Only S14 has an accepted crosswalk overlapping a generated representative: reach 20450127 (also registered Site B), status `SUPPORTED`. On 2014-06-25, its *F. sultana* concentration was `1.3338877637558446e-17 mol/L` and was recorded as detected; S1 observation 4 was also detected at `1.2983219767633366e-17 mol/L`. These are real historical observations, not a follow-up experiment generated by FreshWater.

The observations do **not** identify the organism's biological source zone, reveal what would have happened at C or D under the proposed follow-up, or independently verify binary negative outcomes. S14's crosswalk still needs independent watercourse confirmation. Thus correct-source identification, sampling efficiency, and improvement over another field strategy are **not validated** by this overlap. The Carraro study measured eDNA at 15 sites over time; its purpose and sampling design are distinct from this counterfactual decision test. [Carraro et al., PNAS 2018](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1813843115).

## Reproducible planning comparison and benefit measurements

The generated comparison has four representatives, all score 2. A deterministic baseline that chooses the **nearest generated representative to A** chooses reach 20446568 (2.286 km), also scoring 2. There is no measured discrimination advantage for the current topology rule in this Wigger example. A random-choice baseline over these four representatives has the same score for every choice; no probability or savings claim follows. The registered-site decision has three tied options, so it does not reduce the field plan to a single site.

The JSON output deliberately stores `null` for analyst time, field samples, field and laboratory cost, and correct-source identification. The [empty measurement template](scientific_validation_outputs/benchmark_measurements_template.csv) accepts investigator-supplied times, candidate and sample counts, and separate field, travel, laboratory, and analytical costs. Run `./venv/bin/python scripts/benchmark_sampling.py path/to/measured_runs.csv` to calculate per-workflow medians and total recorded costs. Empty input yields `NO_MEASUREMENTS`; the tool does not calculate percentage savings or source accuracy. No Wigger measurements have been supplied.

To measure practical benefit prospectively, predefine an investigation objective and compare the same cases under FreshWater, nearest-upstream, and unaided planning workflows. Record start/end times for source-zone mapping and plan production; candidate sites inspected; actual samples and replicates; travel, field, laboratory, and analytical costs supplied by each team; assay outcomes; and any independently established source truth. Stratify by watershed and investigator, and preserve protocol versions. Only then calculate comparative times, costs, or success rates. No percentage improvement or cost saving is claimed here.

## Environmental context

The general case-scoped provider architecture has an official [GBIF occurrence-search adapter](https://techdocs.gbif.org/en/openapi/v1/occurrence) and an explicitly pinned ERA5 historical-weather adapter through the Open-Meteo archive. Weather records retain requested dates, daily values, units, returned grid coordinates, retrieval time, 0.25-degree source resolution, missing-data state, and limitations. They remain `UNKNOWN` for compatibility and `UNASSESSED` for evidence strength. Mocked integration tests exercise the Wigger observation date but use fixture coordinates and synthetic weather values; they are functional tests, not Wigger weather observations. The deterministic Wigger investigation still contains **zero collected context records** and does not invoke environmental providers. GHSL historical built-up tiles exist, but a reproducible buffer extraction and raster provisioning path is not configured, so urbanization remains unavailable. [GHSL data package](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/documents/GHSL_Data_Package_2023.pdf).

Occurrence, built-up, rainfall, and temperature data can guide where to inspect or monitor next. They do not independently prove local organism presence, tributary source, pollution, or disease. A future evidence rule would need validation against matched field observations before such context could change source-hypothesis status.

## One Health interpretation

The verified finding is *F. sultana* eDNA at S1. Published work establishes that *F. sultana* can host *Tetracapsuloides bryosalmonae*, and that the parasite is associated with proliferative kidney disease in salmonids. [Morris and Adams, 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17205353/); [Sudhagar et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31877926/). This makes targeted parasite testing and fish-health monitoring potentially relevant. The Wigger *F. sultana* detection itself establishes **neither** local parasite presence **nor** fish infection, disease, human-health risk, or community impact. Those conclusions need parasite assays, fish-health observations, and reviewed environmental and exposure evidence.

## Required external validation

1. Confirm station-to-reach mappings and candidate accessibility in the field.
2. Collect a prospective, replicated follow-up campaign at competing sites with assay controls and complete metadata.
3. Establish independent source truth or a defensible reference standard before reporting source-identification accuracy.
4. Measure workflow time and actual cost under matched comparison protocols.
5. Configure and quality-check dated GHSL, weather, and GBIF extracts if environmental context is needed.
6. Obtain scientific review before promoting the report from Draft.
